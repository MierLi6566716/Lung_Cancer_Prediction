Understanding Lung Cancer Risk Patterns

An exploratory analysis of demographic, behavioral, and symptomatic factors associated with lung cancer diagnosis

Connor Whitlow & Mier Li • Survey Analysis • N = 309 individuals

Study Overview

Lung cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Understanding the interplay between risk factors—from smoking and environmental exposures to symptomatic presentations—is crucial for early detection and prevention strategies.

This analysis examines survey data from 309 individuals to explore patterns in lung cancer diagnosis across multiple dimensions: demographic characteristics (age, gender), behavioral risk factors (smoking, alcohol consumption), chronic conditions, and a comprehensive symptom profile including respiratory distress, fatigue, and other clinical indicators.

Our goal is to move beyond simple bivariate comparisons and examine how risk factors combine and interact. By visualizing cumulative symptom burden, age-risk relationships, and multifactor patterns, we aim to build intuition around which combinations of factors most strongly associate with lung cancer presence in this sample.

Important Note: This is a case-control enriched dataset, not a population-representative sample. The prevalence we observe reflects the study design rather than true population rates. Our focus is on comparative patterns—how risk factors differ between those with and without lung cancer—rather than absolute prevalence estimation.

Research Questions

1. Demographic Patterns
How do age and gender distributions differ between individuals with and without lung cancer?
2. Behavioral Risk Factors
What is the relative magnitude of risk associated with smoking versus alcohol consumption?
3. Symptom Burden
Does cumulative symptom count show a dose-response relationship with lung cancer presence?
4. Multifactor Interactions
How do age, symptom burden, and smoking status jointly differentiate cancer cases from controls?
